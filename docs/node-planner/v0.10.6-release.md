# NodeScope 0.10.6 tester build

> Not released. Its contents ship in 0.11.0; see [v0.11.0-release.md](v0.11.0-release.md).

Prepared from `feature/candidate-sites-0.10.3`: iOS build 2 and Android versionCode 27.
These artifacts are for tester distribution; preparing them does not upload or publish them.

## What to test

- Open Node Planner → Estimated Coverage (Beta). Place a transmitter with a long press, coordinates, or current location; review the radio and receiver assumptions and calculate coverage.
- Tap the coverage map. The centered sample card shows predicted RX, margin after reserve, and grid spacing. Unknown areas show no usable prediction. Close the card, tap another sample, and tap outside the study.
- Tap known nodes and the transmitter. Android now shows their details on both Google Maps and MapLibre. Confirm that a long press still places the transmitter.
- Check sample-card placement above the collapsed controls, map credits, and Undo. Try light/dark appearance, large text, portrait/landscape, and tablet layouts.
- Cancel and continue calculations, change inputs, and calculate again. Old sample selections should clear when results change.
- Save and reopen Link Profile and coverage studies. Check saved inputs and saved results, calculation reports, and the coverage legend.
- Check the filled calculation and supporting-action buttons in Estimated Coverage, Link Profile, Saved Plans, and Node Check.
- What's New: updating from 0.10.5 shows a one-time What's New sheet (iOS) or dialog (Android). Tapping Estimated Coverage or Saved Plans opens it under Explore → Node Planner. A fresh install doesn't show it, it doesn't show again on the next launch, and Settings → What's New reopens it. Content lives in `whats-new.json`, one copy per platform, which a test keeps identical.

Coverage is a sampled estimate using assumed receiver equipment, not measured reception or a guarantee of connectivity.

## TestFlight — What to Test

This build adds Estimated Coverage (Beta) and Saved Plans to Node Planner. Please try:

• What’s New. Updating from 0.10.5 should show it once. Tap an item to jump to it; relaunch and it shouldn’t come back. Settings → What’s New reopens it.
• Explore → Node Planner → Estimated Coverage. Place a transmitter with a long press, on a known node, or at your location. Check the radio and receiver settings, then calculate. Does the map match what you know about coverage there?
• Tap the coverage map to check the predicted signal at a spot, and tap known nodes and the transmitter.
• A large study. If a batch stops early, Continue calculation should finish it. Change only your transmit power and the result should update straight away.
• Save a coverage study and a Link Profile, then reopen them from Saved Plans. Results should come back without recalculating.
• Candidate Sites, now inside Saved Plans.
• On iPad: sidebars start at the top, and the map runs behind the live packets sidebar. On the Mac: ⌘1–⌘5, ⌘F and ⌘R.

Coverage is an estimate from terrain data and the receiver you assume, not measured reception.

## App Store — What's New

New: Estimated Coverage (Beta). Place a transmitter anywhere, on a known node, at your location or with a long press, and see where it’s likely to be heard, based on terrain and your radio settings. Tap the map to check the predicted signal at any spot. Large studies can be continued, and changing only your radio settings updates the result straight away.

New: Saved Plans. Save link profiles and coverage studies, results included, and pick up where you left off. Candidate Sites keeps proposed locations and equipment together in deployment plans. Find it all in Explore → Node Planner.

Better on iPad and Mac: sidebars start at the top of the panel, the map runs behind the live packets sidebar, and on the Mac ⌘1–⌘5 switch tabs, ⌘F searches and ⌘R refreshes.

After an update, NodeScope now shows what’s new. Reopen it anytime from Settings.

Coverage is an estimate from terrain data and the receiver you assume, not a guarantee of real-world reception.
